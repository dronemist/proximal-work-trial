Folio Nº 03 · North Atlantic Holdings

The Cormorant House, a tide-watcher's retreat on Island Wall.

Stewarded by Eleanor Whitfield
Since the spring of 2018

A two-bedroom Edwardian fisherman's house on the north flank of Island Wall, painted hull-black, with a green slate roof and a long, quiet kitchen that opens onto a courtyard of espaliered pear. The eastern view holds the harbour wall and, on a clear morning, the white promise of France.

Chapter I · The House

A measured restoration, three storeys from the harbour wall.

Built in 1907 by a Whitstable oyster-merchant and lightly altered ever since, the house was returned to its full Edwardian plan in 2019 by the architect Anna Brest. The original press, the smoke pantry, and the steep boxed stair are all preserved. The flooring is reclaimed pine — the deck of a French fishing boat, dismantled at Calais — and the walls are pigmented lime, mixed by hand on a kitchen table to a colour she calls "weatherboard, after rain."

There are two bedrooms, one a half-storey above the other, and a small back room that the previous owner called The Watchroom — a single window seat, and shelves enough for fourteen field guides. Guests describe it as the room they did not know they needed.

Chapter II

A room that reads the weather.

The upstairs sitting room is a sixteen-pane lantern glazed on three sides and a half to the floor. The light comes in low, even on a winter afternoon, and the slow grain of the spruce ceiling holds it like a held breath. Sit here with the field glasses and the right book and the morning will pass without notice.

A spotting scope on a brass tripod stands in the south-east corner — the better, the previous owner believed, to know one's neighbours by their habit on the water.

Chapter III

The library belongs to whoever wakes first.

A wall of soft-painted shelves runs the length of the landing, holding a working library of natural history, maritime memoir, and the slow novels of the inter-war years. Borrow what you wish; leave behind what you read. A small ledger in the front hall keeps track of the conversation.

Chapter V · The Host

Hosted by Eleanor Whitfield since 2018.

Eleanor lives a slow ten minutes' walk from the house, in the cottage with the green door and the rosemary growing in a pot the size of a bucket. She is the third generation of her family on the wall; she keeps a small private notebook of weather, tides, and the moments she'd like a guest not to miss.

Eleanor Whitfield
Whitstable, England · Responds in 38 min · 124 stays hosted

Chapter VIII · House notes

A few quiet expectations.

Of an evening, the lights ought to be low.
It is a small house in a careful row; the lantern in the front garden is held to a low setting after ten in the evening.
A maximum of one quiet dog, on the ledger.
Eleanor will ask, kindly, that you write the name and habit of your dog in the front hall ledger on arrival.
No more than four to dinner at the long oak table.
A house for a careful little party. If you intend to have friends over, please let your host know on the day of arrival.
A pot of tea, when you leave, on the front step.
Eleanor will collect the last pot of tea on her way to the wharf, and leave a note in your place. A small ritual the house has carried since 1998.

Reserve The Cormorant House.

Four nights is a comfortable minimum. The house keeps its own keys and its own light; the host will meet you on the day, just before the harbour bell.
