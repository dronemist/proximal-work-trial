Reading No. 02

A summer held its breath, and the rooms stayed full.

Mid-August leaned hot. Around it, the booking-graph pulls upward: 12 of 13 properties booked above the four-year median between Whitsun and Lammas, with the long mid-August week reaching a sustained 96.1% occupied. The Cormorant House and Kuro Moka, both of which closed for one week in July for maintenance, recovered immediately on opening.

The portfolio ranks itself, top to bottom, by quiet preference.

RevPAR has stayed remarkably even across the year. The Cormorant House sits highest at €445 — the result of higher rate-floors and slow growth in repeat stays. At the lower end, the Vauclause Studio at €148; not a struggle, simply a smaller room.

Where the bookings come from, and what that says.

Direct reservations through the studio's own pages still carry the largest share — 43% — driven by repeat guests and word-of-mouth. Airbnb makes up a steady 28%, with Plum Guide and Folio of London comprising the rest. We continue to recommend no fewer than three channels, no more than five.

Folio Endnote

What the figures are not saying.

A reporting period is a narrow window. The studio's largest tact — the patience it asks of its hosts, and the slow construction of return — does not show up here in any direct way. The numbers above show the lift; the letters in our drawer, the readers behind it.

Two new properties, December's Fjord-Side and the May reopening of the Old Wool Store, will reset the bottom rows here. Until then: rest, repair, write.
