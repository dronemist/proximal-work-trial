Folio No. 07·Coastal·Island Wall

The Cormorant House, a tide-watcher's retreat on Island Wall.

A three-bedroom stone cottage on the western reach of Island Wall, where the sea fog drifts in low and the harbour bell rings on the half-hour. Renovated in 2022 with a careful hand for original timber, original tile, and a kitchen that knows what to do with mackerel.

Chapter 1 — The room

A measured restoration, three stones from the harbour wall.

Built in 1873 for a Norwegian quarry foreman, restored in 2022 by the studio at Olm Lethier. The kitchen window watches the boats unload, the bedroom windows watch the rooks settle, and the back garden ends a stone's throw from the breakwater. The terms are simple: linen on the bed, almonds on the table, and salt in the wind.

A library of two hundred books, mostly fiction and field guides, lines the entrance hall. The wood stove draws best in the small hours; we leave a basket of split alder at the door.

Chapter 2

A room that reads the weather.

The parlour faces the western horizon. In late summer, the room is washed gold by six; in November the same light arrives by three. We leave a notebook on the table; guests note the colour of the harbour and the names of their visiting birds.

Chapter 3

The library belongs to whoever wakes first.

Two hundred volumes — a small but specific collection. Field guides, Scandinavian fiction, and a complete set of Olfgang Powis. Generally we ask that books go back on the shelves where they came from, but no-one will check.

The conditions of the visit
A few quiet expectations.
Check-in
From 4:00 p.m. Eleanor meets new guests at the door, or, if our schedules dictate otherwise, the key is left in a tin box in the alder bin.
Check-out
By 11:00 a.m. Strip the beds if you like, but no need. Lock the front; leave the courtyard door ajar for the cat.
Quiet hours
10 p.m. to 7 a.m. We share the wall with two neighbours, both of whom rise early to fish.
Cancellation
Full refund up to 30 days before arrival. After that, the first three nights remain billed; the rest is returned with grace.
Children
Welcome from age five. The stairs are old and steep; the wood stove warm. The garden wall does not need vigilance, but children do.
