CORMORANT · OPEN · 4 GUESTS

The Cormorant House, a tide-watcher's retreat on Island Wall.

A four-bedroom Edwardian fisherman's cottage on the gravel spit at Whitstable, with deep-set windows looking out across the oyster beds and the railhead at Tankerton. Edward Lear's salt cabinets, salt-air herringbone floors.

THE HOUSE

A measured restoration, three streets from the harbour wall.

Built in 1894 by a Whitstable oyster broker and partly rebuilt in 1958 after the floods, the Cormorant has been with us since 2017. We took it on with the brief of restraint — no new doorways cut, no light fittings replaced, no pictures hung above the wainscot. What followed was, in the end, more of an editing than a restoration: an inventory of what should stay.

Inside, the rooms have been hung with linen the colour of beach grass and shells of pinned mussel and salt-bleached oak. The kitchen is a single galley of marble and copper. The library, on the half-landing, looks out over the strand and the railway line beyond.

NOTE FROM THE BOOK

A room that reads the weather.

The upstairs landing window faces east, and on bright mornings you'll find a patchwork of clear glass on the rugs. By afternoon the rooms have turned more honeyed; by evening, when the lamps come on, they take on the same gold as the lighthouse keep at Whistable Bay.

FROM THE STUDY

The library belongs to whoever wakes first.

Books on Kent, on tides, on Aldous Huxley and on Roger Deakin — and, at the foot of the divan, an early Penguin Lear that came with the house. The kettle is downstairs but the coffee press is here. Pull the curtains, light the lamp.
