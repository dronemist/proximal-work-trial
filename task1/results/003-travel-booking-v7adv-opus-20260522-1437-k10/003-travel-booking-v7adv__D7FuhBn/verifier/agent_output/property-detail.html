The Cormorant House, a tide-watcher's retreat on Island Wall.

A four-bedroom Edwardian fisherman's house, restored in 2022 with chalk-lime plaster, salvaged Suffolk oak, and a study filled with weather almanacs. Booked by writers, tide-pool readers, and travellers between Skye and Stornoway.

Chapter One — The House

A measured restoration, three streets from the harbour wall.

Built in 1894, refitted in 2022 with chalk-lime plaster, oak floors salvaged from a Suffolk longhouse, and Belgian linen curtains, the house keeps the bones of the original. The hand-painted blue and red trim on the kitchen door — an inheritance of the original carpenter — has been carefully preserved by a conservation cabinet-maker from Kintyre.

Of the four bedrooms, two face the bay; one faces an inner courtyard of climbing roses; and one looks across the slate roofs of Island Wall toward the lighthouse. The kitchen is fitted in lime-washed pine, with a single, well-loved Lacanche range. The library — small, with cushions, and full of weather almanacs from the 1930s onward — looks east, and gathers the first hour of morning sun.

Chapter Two

A room that reads the weather.

The garden room is a 1.5-square-metre addition glassed on three sides and roofed in old-pattern lead. In summer it serves as a sun-thaw; in winter, a reading porch with a wood stove and three lambswool throws. Newspaper subscriptions arrive Tuesday and Friday by post.

Chapter Three

The library belongs to whoever wakes first.

Built into the half-landing between floors, the library holds two armchairs and a sliding ladder. The shelves carry books on tide patterns, North Atlantic weather, Hebrides folklore, and a small collection of long-out-of-print Penguin orange spines.
