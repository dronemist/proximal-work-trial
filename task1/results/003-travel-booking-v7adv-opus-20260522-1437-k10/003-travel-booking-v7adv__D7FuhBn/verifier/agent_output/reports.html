Quarterly Summary — Q3 2024

The quarter, read in figures and in feeling.

A measured account of how the portfolio performed between July and September — occupancy held by season, average rates lifted by the deliberate omission of cheaper nights, and a guestbook that reads, this year, like a private letter.

In feeling

A summer held its breath, and the rooms stayed full.

Mid-July through Mid-September held above 88%. Heavy travel was bookable Mediterranean — Provence and Andalucía — while the Northern houses (Hebrides, Bornholm) saw quieter, slow-stayed bookings, with longer length of stay and a softer arrival.

Reading Two

Rates lifted without strain, the market simply followed.

The mid-summer rate increase, posted in February, lifted ADR without softening occupancy. Direct channels accepted the increase first; channel partners followed by April.

In feeling

The portfolio ranks itself, top to bottom, by quiet preference.

The colder houses — those off the railway — earn more per night by deliberate scarcity, less by competing on price. The midsummer thaw favours the southern catalogue, though the Hebrides quietly leads on length-of-stay.

Reading Six

What the figures are not saying.

A reporting period is a narrow window. The studio's longer view — the years on either side — is what gives the quarterly numbers their meaning. The increase in ADR has not, this far, cost us guests; the median length of stay has, in fact, lengthened by half a night. The houses go quieter in November and that is, on balance, by design.

This was deliberate. Maison Verre will close for ten days at the new year for the longer kitchen restoration.
