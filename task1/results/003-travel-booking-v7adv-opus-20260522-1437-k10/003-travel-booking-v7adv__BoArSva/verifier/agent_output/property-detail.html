Folio №07 · Island Wall, Maine

The Cormorant House,
a tide-watcher's retreat on Island Wall.

A small, weather-tight cottage on the lee side of a granite headland, with a wide porch and the smell of juniper. Built in 1932 by Niels Aarendal for his sister; restored last year by the studio after an absence of seventeen.

Chapter 1 · Overview

A measured restoration, three storeys from the harbour wall.

Built in 1932 by Niels Aarendal for his sister Inge — a watercolourist who painted on the porch each morning — the cottage was restored last summer by the studio. The floorboards, the cast iron stove, the rope-pulled lamps in the bedrooms: all original. The kitchen, the bath, the linens are new.

There are three bedrooms across the upper floors; the largest faces the harbour. A wood stove warms the parlour; a thin rail of stairs leads to the cellar where the wine is kept. The garden, half wild now, holds two beach plums and a copse of bayberry.

Chapter 3 · Mornings

A room that reads the weather.

The porch faces east and gathers the first light by half-six. By seven, the kettle is on. The wide window over the kitchen sink frames a working harbour — lobster boats, the slow figure of the Sunday ferry to Vinalhaven. A quiet civic life proceeds without you.

Chapter 4 · The Shelves

The library belongs to whoever wakes first.

Built into the hall, twelve feet long and packed with the leavings of three generations: Walter Scott, an unaccountable run of mid-century cookbooks, two atlases, a copy of Robert Frost spotted with age. We ask only that you reshelve as you find them, more or less.

Chapter 6 · The Host

Hosted by Eleanor Whitfield since 2019.

Eleanor grew up two harbours east, in a town called Penrith. She runs the Cormorant with patience and an unhurried hand — the keys hang on a brass hook by the door; the cellar is full; the wood is split. If she does not answer your message within the hour, it is because she is on the water.
