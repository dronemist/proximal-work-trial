FOLIO No. 04 / ISLAND WALL

The Cormorant House, a tide-watcher's retreat on Island Wall.

A two-bedroom Edwardian fisherman's lodge perched on the limestone shoulder of Island Wall, restored over four winters with a printer's eye for register and the dust of an estuary lent back to it. Sleeps four, listens to all weather.

PROPERTY 04 OF 11
CHAPTER I · ABOUT THE HOUSE

A measured restoration, three streets from the harbour wall.

Built in 1909 by a Whitstable oyster merchant, the house was occupied in the same family for ninety-two years before our trust took ownership in 2019. Three winters of careful work have stripped two layers of plaster, returned the dressers to their original walnut, and reinstated the open hearth in the front room. The kitchen looks east and is at its best at six in the morning.

CHAPTER II · ON THE LIBRARY

A room that reads the weather.

The upstairs library holds 312 hand-bound volumes — chosen by Eleanor, the house's earlier curator. It is reorganised every spring, ordered by atmosphere rather than alphabet: north light, south light, the swell of fog, the swell of memory. Guests are invited to lend the books they read while staying, and to leave one of their own behind.

CHAPTER III · ON STEWARDSHIP

The library belongs to whoever wakes first.

Both kitchen and library face the same low light at six. We've never asked guests to choose between them — the kettle is loud either way and we keep the espresso for those who'd rather not converse before nine. Most return to the kitchen by 8:40, when the bakery delivery arrives at the side door.
