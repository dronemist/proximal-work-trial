Chapter I

A summer held its breath, and the rooms stayed full.

Mid-August briefly tested 94% occupancy across the bookable inventory. We declined three bookings in the week of the Festival of Tides — to protect the rhythm of guests we'd already promised — and absorbed the cost. We'll do it again, but quieter.

Chapter II

Rates lifted without strain, the market simply followed.

A modest increase of the published rate at peak — €25 a night across four properties — produced no resistance, and a noticeable lift in revenue per available room. We'll hold rates through October before adjusting again at year-end.

Chapter III

The portfolio ranks itself, top to bottom, by quiet preference.

Maison Verre held its lead — early sun, the orchard view, the way the kitchen smells of dried bay. The Atelier slipped slightly: a new neighbour is renovating, and we may need to revisit the rate.

Channels

Where the bookings come from, and what that says.

Direct bookings — through the studio's own pages and the rare envelope of a postcard — now contribute 38% of revenue at considerably better margins. We won't ever turn the marketplaces off, but we are quietly thinning the channels.

Closing Notes

What the figures are not yet saying.

A reporting period is a narrow window. The studio's larger arc — five years in to a ten-year reading — is hardly visible in these returns. We've added two long-cycle metrics underneath: lifetime guest value, and a rolling thirty-six month occupancy moving average. We'd suggest reading both, slowly, the way one might a barometer at dusk.

The next statement, December's, will close the year and the book.
