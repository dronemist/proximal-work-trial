Folio No. 03 · Coastal · Cormorants

The Cormorant House, a tide-watcher's retreat on Island Wall.

A four-bedroom Edwardian fisherman's house, restored in 2022 with chalk-lime plaster, salvaged Suffolk oak, and a kelp-fibre dyed plain palette of greys and bone — a house that listens long before it answers, like its namesake.

Photography by Anders Brand · Last updated 12 May 2025
A note from the host

A measured restoration, three streets from the harbour wall.

Built in 1908, originally a fish-buyer's house with a salting room on the ground floor, the Cormorant House sat empty for thirty years before we took it on. The restoration kept the steep stair, the lime-washed kitchen and a single coal-grate; everything else was added with the lightest hand we could manage. There are two writing desks. The bath fills slowly, on purpose.

The garden, a long thin slip of brick and rosemary, faces south and warms by mid-morning. From the upper bedroom you can see the lighthouse at Sole Bay turning its slow green eye.

Chapter III

A room that reads the weather.

The upper landing is fitted with a barometer, a tide table and a small folio of Beaufort scale notes — kept by an ancestor of the house. Guests read them like a daily paper, and many leave a line of their own.

Chapter IV

The library belongs to whoever wakes first.

Built into the half-landing between floors, the library wraps three walls in oak shelves, mostly maritime poetry, regional histories and a curious collection of old hymnals. There is a rocking chair, an oilskin lamp, and a thermos kept stoppered with cork. We ask only that you turn the spine to the wall when you find a favourite, so the next guest finds it newly.
