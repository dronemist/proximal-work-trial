READING THE WEEK

A summer held its breath, and the rooms stayed full.

Mid-July to mid-August saw a high tide — 11 of the eleven properties booked above 89% across that period. The fairer August nights, week 33, briefly slipped on the cusp of Atlantic weather, and a quiet week followed.

The shoulder weeks of late September — long known as our calmest — closed at 64%, a notable rise from a year ago.

RATES, IN PROFILE

Rates lifted without strain, the market simply followed.

The third quarter announced a price-rise of 9.4% across the portfolio, without a corresponding fall in occupancy. With a market 6.3% lower over the same period — by which we read the index of the regional press — we treated the quarter as a kind of editorial confirmation. Hold the line in Q4.

PORTFOLIO READING

The portfolio ranks itself, top to bottom, by quiet preference.

Atelier sur la Galange — the smallest property — earned the highest yield per available night, by some margin. The longer Cabaña Pintoresca held its August nights at €278 sets the floor that smaller houses follow, and the broader fixed-cost band is starting to tell.

Atelier Nord, our newest of the eleven, will require a slow second look at autumn pricing.

CHANNEL MIX

Where the bookings come from, and what that says.

Direct reservations through the studio's pages crossed the half-line this quarter — 52% — for the first time since 2021. Mr & Mrs Smith remains a thoughtful second channel; Airbnb a useful, if increasingly seasoned, third.

CLOSING WORDS

What the figures are not yet saying.

A reporting period is a coarse machine. The studio's longer arc — five properties to eleven over six years, a calendar that now runs eighteen months ahead, a steady migration toward direct bookings — is what the studio steers by. Q3, in that light, was a good one. Q4 will be a quieter one.

The next statement, December's, will close the year and the half-decade.
