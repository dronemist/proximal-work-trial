FOLIO No. 03 COASTAL ISLAND WALL, KENT

The Cormorant House, a tide-watcher's retreat on Island Wall.

A four-bedroom Edwardian fisherman's house, restored in 1923, with crab-grass borders, salt-grey timber, and a view from every kitchen window. Sleeps eight comfortably, ten in a pinch.

Check availability
CHAPTER I

A measured restoration, three streets from the harbour wall.

Built in 1909, the Cormorant House was returned to itself between 2018 and 2023 by the architect Joana Vidal and her studio in Margate. The salt-stripped clapboard was re-clad in tarred larch, the rooms re-plastered with horsehair lime, and the kitchen built around a single oak table that seats ten. Bookings begin at three nights — short enough to be a long weekend, long enough to learn the names of the lifeboat crew.

ROOM 02

A room that reads the weather.

The upper room is a 14-square-metre rebellion against the open plan — a single bed, a deep window-sill, a writing desk that overlooks the bird-loud lane.

ROOM 03

The library belongs to whoever wakes first.

Built into the half-landing between floors, the library is a quiet, lamp-lit room of about six square metres. Best in autumn, when the rain begins to take the slate.
