02Operating thesis

Methodology paper →

We finance the slow work of pulling landscapes back to function. Not gardens, not parks. Working tracts of ground — tidal, riparian, forested, peat — that can hold carbon, slow water, and house the species they used to. The foundry's mandate is unusual in three ways.

First, we ledger every disbursement publicly, at the milestone level, on the day it clears. Second, we commit only to six-year measurement windows: no project enters our portfolio without an explicit, signed plan for what we will measure, on what cadence, and what would cause us to withdraw funding. Third, we publish failure data — the project pages carry the misses alongside the hits, on the same axis.

None of this is novel; it is just slow, and unglamorous. Most restoration capital still moves on five-figure tranches, on undefined timelines, to organizations that aren't equipped to measure outcomes for more than 18 months. We are trying to be the boring exception.

03Allocation of the foundry's mandate

Allocation paper →
L

Land first, narrative second

We hold ground only when we hold the land. No sites enter the portfolio unless title, lease, or covenant is recorded and tenable for thirty years.

M

Evidence against optimism

We commit to measuring what would prove us wrong, not what would prove us right. Failure axes are signed before disbursement.

F

No greenwashing instruments

The foundry does not issue, sell, or steward voluntary carbon credits. Capital is donated, granted, or spent — never traded.

P

Public ledger or it didn't happen

If a disbursement is not on the ledger by close of the next business day, it has not, for our purposes, occurred. No exceptions.
