02FOUNDRY THESISRead time · 3 min

We finance the slow work of getting landscape back in function. Our genres — saltmarsh, peatland, broadleaf, kelp — have one thing in common: restoration takes a working generation to settle and a working century to fully express. Most philanthropic capital is impatient with that timescale.

So the foundry holds capital in tenure. Donors give once; the foundry stewards across decades. We publish the ledger. The board includes two of our wardens. There is no executive bonus and no marketing department, and we are not trying to grow.

The pages that follow are a working accounting of where the foundry's capital has gone, what it grew or did not, and how we report the difference.

04WHAT THE FOUNDRY DOESFour-fold
a · Tenure

Land first, narrative second

We take in unrestricted gifts and deploy them on durable land tenure — long leases, restoration covenants, or freehold purchase for the very small slice held in trust.

b · Cohorts

Evidence-set against academia

Every cohort is paired with a partnered university lab. Forecast bands are published before planting; year-three results are mapped against them.

c · Hands

Local hands, local payroll

Eleven wardens, two ecologists, and a treasurer are on year-round salary. We don't subcontract critical labour because labour writes the data.

d · Reporting

No greenwashing instruments

No credit issuance, no NFTs, no "rewards." Public ledger, signed independent audit, and the print prospectus you can hold in your hand.
