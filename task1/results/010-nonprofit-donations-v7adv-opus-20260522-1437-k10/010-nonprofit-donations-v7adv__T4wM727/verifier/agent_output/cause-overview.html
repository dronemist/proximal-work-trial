§ 02

The Foundry, In Brief

We finance the slow work of pulling landscapes back to function. Not gardens, not garden suburbs — salt marsh, riparian floodplain, peatland, scrub, mature temperate woodland. We grant in tranches against measurable milestones; we audit on twelve-month cadences; we publish results, line by line, as the work clears.

We have no general fundraising budget. We have one part-time press officer. The rest of our overhead is roughly seven percent — the remainder is granted out at hectare-level cost lines that anyone, donor or critic, can interrogate.

The Foundry was incorporated in 2018 by E. Brae, M. Holloway, and the Cartwright Bequest. It is governed by a five-seat board, two of whom are restoration ecologists, two of whom are donors, and one of whom is appointed by the Carpentaria Land Council.

§ 06

Charter Excerpt

Filed 2018 · Last amended 2023
§ 1 Object
To restore degraded ecosystems — tidal, peat, riparian, woodland — under transparently audited contracts of not less than ten years.
§ 2 Scope
Direct contracting only. No granting to general environmental campaigning bodies. No re-granting through intermediaries except where strictly necessary.
§ 3 Ledger
Every payment exceeding £500 published in plain text, with project ID, beneficiary, date, and milestone — within 21 days.
§ 4 Audit
Annual external audit by a firm chosen on rotation. Field audits at every project, no less often than every 24 months.
§ 5 Governance
Five-seat board. Quorum three. Conflicts to be declared in the public minute book.
§ 6 Dissolution
On dissolution, all assets to be transferred to a successor body of charitable kind, subject to court approval.

§ 07

Restoration Themes

Six standing programmes
▣ Theme 01

Land first, narrative second

We will not capitalise on contracts we cannot defend in front of the field crew. Every project, every site, walked annually by board members.

▣ Theme 02

Permanence by covenant

We refuse projects whose protection runs less than the lifecycle of the species we aim to recover. Sphagnum and oak both outlive our generation.

▣ Theme 03

Endowment against capture

Donors do not name boards. Donors do not pick projects. A bequest pays for restoration outcomes, not for influence over how the trust thinks.

▣ Theme 04

No greenwashing instruments

The Foundry has chosen, by charter, not to issue or trade carbon offsets, biodiversity units, or any nature-derived financial instrument.

▣ Theme 05

Local hands, local pay

Where the land sits, the contracts go. Field crews are recruited within forty miles of every project. Pay is published annually.

▣ Theme 06

Public ledger as it ledger as it happens

We file the books in plain prose. Books are not for accountants — the books are for the public who funded the work.
